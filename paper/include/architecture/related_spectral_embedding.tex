\subsection{Spectral Methods and Manifold Structure}
\label{sec:related_spectral_embedding}

\paragraph{Laplacian Eigenmaps and Manifold Learning.}
Belkin and Niyogi's Laplacian Eigenmaps \citep{belkin2003laplacian}
established that the eigenvectors of the graph Laplacian $L = D - W$
provide optimal low-dimensional embeddings that preserve local
neighborhood structure.  The $k$ smallest non-trivial eigenvectors
minimize the embedding distortion $\sum_{ij} w_{ij} \| y_i - y_j
\|^2$, placing similar nodes close together in the embedding space.

The Value architecture offers an implicit analogue.  Values derived from
similar input byte sequences occupy nearby points in $\{0,1\}^{8192}$
under the Hamming metric, and the population-count inner product provides
a measure of neighborhood similarity.  A graph defined by thresholding
this similarity would have a Laplacian whose spectral properties reflect
the structure of the data.  While the current system does not perform
explicit eigendecomposition, the geometric structure of the Value space
is consistent with what spectral methods would recover: inputs that share
byte-level structure are embedded nearby, and the boolean operations
applied during Region mixing correspond to local transformations in this
space.

\paragraph{The Dirac Operator and Distance Computation.}
Knill's formulation of the Dirac operator on graphs
\citep{knill2013dirac} provides a discrete analogue of the continuous
Dirac operator whose square yields the Laplacian.  The spectrum of this
operator encodes both the topology (via its kernel, related to Betti
numbers) and the geometry (via its non-zero eigenvalues) of the graph,
enabling geodesic distance computation from spectral data without
explicit shortest-path algorithms.

In the Value architecture, distances between frames are computed directly
through population-count operations: the Hamming distance between two
Values counts the number of bit positions at which they differ, and the
Hamming inner product (the population count of their bitwise AND) measures
their overlap.  These operations execute in constant time per frame and
produce distance estimates consistent with the spectral framework, though
they do not require constructing or decomposing a graph Laplacian.

\paragraph{Emergent Manifold Structure.}
Whether the collection of Values produced by processing a given dataset
exhibits non-trivial manifold structure---such as periodicity or toroidal
geometry---is an empirical question that depends on the structure of the
input data.  If the input contains periodic or cyclical patterns (for
example, temporal sequences with repeating motifs), these patterns will
manifest as regularities in the Hamming geometry of the resulting Values.
The phase coherence experiments in the experimental sections provide
initial measurements of whether such structure is detectable.
