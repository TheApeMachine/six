\subsection{Text Classification}
\label{sec:text_classification}

\paragraph{Task Description.}
The text classification experiment evaluates zero-shot topical categorisation
on the AG News dataset (\texttt{sh0416/ag\_news}).  Articles from four
categories---World, Sports, Business, and Science/Technology---are ingested
with their label appended.  At test time the label suffix is stripped via
substring holdout; the system must recover the correct category word from the
closest resident labelled article in the substrate.

\paragraph{Results.}
Table~\ref{tab:text_classification_metrics} summarises the classification
metrics across $N = 100$ test samples.
The confusion matrix is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:text_classification_confusion}.

\paragraph{Assessment.}
Classification accuracy was low.  With only $N = 100$ samples the
substrate may not have built sufficient attractor density to separate all
four AG News categories reliably.  Scaling the ingestion volume is expected
to improve per-class disambiguation.

  \centering
  \caption{Text Classification --- summary metrics.}
  \label{tab:text_classification_metrics}
  \begin{tabular}{ll}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Metric} & \textbf{Value} \\
    \midrule
    Macro-F1          & 24.7\% \\
    Overall Accuracy  & 36.0\% \\
    Balanced Accuracy & 28.3\% \\
    Exact Score       & 36.0\% \\
    Sample Size       & $N = 100$ \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  {\footnotesize
  \textbf{Macro-F1} (from \texttt{experiment/evaluator.go}): unweighted mean of per-class F1 scores derived from the confusion matrix $C$.\par
  \textbf{Overall Accuracy}: $\bigl(\sum_i C_{i,i}\bigr)/N$; every test prompt is counted, and unresolved prompts are misses.\par
  \textbf{Exact Score}: $\bigl(\sum_i C_{i,i}\bigr)/\bigl(\sum_{i,j} C_{i,j}\bigr)$ (diagonal over total confusion-matrix mass).
  It matches Overall Accuracy here because every test sample received a prediction in a valid class index, so $\sum_{i,j} C_{i,j}=N$ and the denominators coincide.\par
  \textbf{Balanced Accuracy}: unweighted mean of per-class recalls using row-normalised diagonal entries of $C$.}
\end{table}
